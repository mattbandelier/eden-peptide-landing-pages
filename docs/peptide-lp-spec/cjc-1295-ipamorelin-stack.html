What Is the CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin Stack?

The stack pairs two peptides that work on the same system from two different angles:

CJC-1295 is a synthetic GHRH analog — Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone. It signals the pituitary to release growth hormone. Eden's protocols typically use the no-DAC form (also called Mod GRF 1-29), which has a short half-life that matches Ipamorelin's pulse timing.

Ipamorelin is a selective growth hormone secretagogue. It binds the ghrelin receptor and triggers a GH pulse — without raising cortisol, prolactin, or aldosterone, which is why it's called the "clean" GH releaser.

Together: CJC-1295 raises baseline GH availability, Ipamorelin generates the actual pulse. The result is a stronger and cleaner overnight GH release than either peptide alone — and it mimics the natural rhythm of the GH system rather than overriding it.

Frequently Asked

What is the CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin stack?

A two-peptide protocol pairing CJC-1295 (a GHRH analog) with Ipamorelin (a selective growth hormone secretagogue) to support a stronger, cleaner overnight GH pulse than either alone.

Is this the same as HGH?

No. HGH is synthetic human growth hormone — you take someone else's GH. CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin stimulates your own pituitary to release more of your own GH, in your body's natural pulse pattern. It's the "support, don't replace" approach.

Is it FDA-approved?

CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin are not FDA-approved drugs. They are compounded under physician prescription by a U.S. licensed pharmacy partner — the same regulatory framework used for many specialty therapies in functional and longevity medicine.

Which form of CJC-1295 does Eden use?

Most Eden protocols use CJC-1295 without DAC (also called Mod GRF 1-29), which has a short half-life that matches Ipamorelin's pulse timing. Your clinician will explain why this matters and whether your protocol uses with-DAC or no-DAC.

How is it administered?

Subcutaneous injection, typically nightly before bed to align with the body's natural overnight GH pulse. First-time users get an in-clinic walkthrough.

How soon do people notice changes?

Sleep depth often shifts in the first 1–2 weeks. Recovery and energy typically follow over 4–8 weeks. Body composition and skin changes are longer — 8–16 weeks — because they're driven by IGF-1 and tissue remodeling, which take time. Individual response varies.

How is eligibility determined?

Through clinical consultation. Your clinician reviews your goals, medical history, current medications, and may request baseline labs including IGF-1 before recommending a protocol.

Can it stack with TRT, GLP-1, or other peptides?

Often, yes. Your clinician will review compatibility during consult. This stack is frequently combined with BPC-157/TB-500 for recovery, or with TRT/HRT for body composition.

What are the side effects?

Most users tolerate the stack well. Reported effects can include mild injection-site reactions, water retention in the first weeks, and vivid dreams (a known effect of stronger deep-sleep cycles). Your clinician reviews your full history at consult.

Will it show on a drug test?

Both peptides are on WADA's prohibited list. Athletes subject to drug testing should screen carefully with their clinician before starting.
